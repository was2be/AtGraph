Overview

@Graph is a macOS application for scientific data: graphs of recordings and other time series, images and image stacks, spreadsheets, and charts, with the measurements, curve fits, and statistics that turn them into results. It is intuitive enough for students and teachers, and rigorous enough for research in academia or industry.

Version 0.9 adds six toolkits, each a set of tools for one field of science: Electrophysiology, Pharmacology & Biochemistry, Biosignals, Spectroscopy, Flow Cytometry, and Astronomy. They come with @Graph at no extra cost, and the results of every toolkit flow into the same graphs, charts, and statistics.

Features

Fast, interactive graphs

Long, multi-channel recordings drawn as sweeps: zoom, place cursors, filter, correct baselines, and measure, with smooth redraws even for large datasets.

Microscopy and imaging

Open Z-series and time-series images, correct uneven illumination, drift, and background, find spots and beads, draw or detect ROIs, and measure ΔF/F0 traces that go straight to graphs. Export movies as MP4, ProRes, or TIFF stacks.

Easy “omics” visualization

Volcano and MA plots, clustered heatmaps, dot plots, UpSet and Venn diagrams, PCA, GSEA, and Manhattan plots, from GCT, Loom, AnnData, 10x Genomics, and GEO files.

Scientific file support

Open the files you already have: neurophysiology and physiology recordings, microscopy images, statistics packages, genomics, flow cytometry, plate readers, spectroscopy, and astronomy. See every file type.

Analysis tools

Filters, baselines, peak detection, curve fitting to any formula, and spectra (FFT, or Lomb–Scargle for unevenly sampled data), with results that go to the Data Log, a spreadsheet, or statistics.

Rigorous statistics

The effect size and its confidence interval come first. Choose what to compare (means, trimmed means, medians, or ranks), and @Graph runs the matching test, checks your data, and suggests alternatives without forcing them. Mixed models handle repeated and nested data and values past a detection limit.

Charts that stay in sync

Bar, box, violin, ridge, line, scatter, survival, and estimation plots, with effect-size brackets drawn on the chart. Every chart has a live spreadsheet: edit one and the other updates.

Batch processing and protocols

Run one analysis on a folder of files and get one report, or save a list of steps as a protocol and run it on any window. The numbers match the inspector’s.

AI Assistant

Ask about your open files in plain English, or tell the assistant what to do. It runs @Graph’s own analyses, so the numbers are computed, not guessed. Use Apple Private Cloud Compute, or Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, or Grok with your own API key.

Lab Tools

Calculators for everyday lab work, from the Nernst equation and dose conversion between species to qPCR efficiency, Beer–Lambert, and airmass.

Export elegant figures and data

Vector PDF and SVG, or PNG, TIFF, JPEG, and GIF; Copy as PDF and Send to Keynote. Save data as ABF, AxoGraph, HDF5, MATLAB, NWB, or CSV.

Native for macOS

Built for Apple silicon, with VoiceOver support and recovery copies of unsaved work, for an intuitive and modern macOS experience.

Toolkits

Six toolkits add the tools of particular fields. All six are free, come with @Graph, and are shown at first: their tools are tabs in the inspector, image tools, windows in the Toolkits menu, and calculators in Lab Tools. Hide the ones you don’t use in Settings › Toolkits; Find a Tool still finds every tool, and Batch Processing and Protocols still run them.

Electrophysiology

Action potential and synaptic event detection, LFP and spike-train analysis, VI and IV plots, firing properties, sag and slow AHPs, spike sorting, membrane tests, and series-resistance (Rs) and junction-potential correction for patch-clamp and extracellular recordings.

Pharmacology & Biochemistry

Dose–response and Schild analysis, enzyme kinetics and binding, initial rates, pharmacokinetics, qPCR and melt curves, Western blot and gel densitometry, plate maps for 6- to 1,536-well plate readers with standard curves and plate quality checks, plates read from a photo, and colony counting on dishes.

Biosignals

ECG intervals and heart-rate variability, breathing and respiratory sinus arrhythmia, EEG band power, and EMG envelopes and bursts, for people and animals from mice to hummingbirds.

Spectroscopy

Spectral baselines, peak fitting with Gaussian, Lorentzian, and pseudo-Voigt shapes, spectra converted between wavelength, wavenumber, energy, and Raman shift, wavelength calibration, mass spectra with ion chromatograms and isotope patterns, and spectra read from a photo of a spectroscope.

Flow Cytometry

A Cytometry window for FCS files and FlowJo workspaces: every event compensated on Logicle and arcsinh axes, gating with population statistics, spillover and spectral unmixing, PeacoQC, FlowSOM and PhenoGraph clusters, UMAP and t-SNE, and differential abundance between groups.

Astronomy

FITS images with sky coordinates and auto stretch, CCD calibration, stacking and color pictures, plate solving with star names under the pointer, aperture photometry, and transit search and fitting.

Privacy

AtGraph LLC receives no data from @Graph: no accounts, analytics, advertising or tracking. Your files stay on your Mac.

The AI Assistant is optional. When you use it, your questions and the data or charts you ask it to work with go directly from your Mac to the provider you choose: Apple Private Cloud Compute, or Anthropic, OpenAI, Google or xAI with your own API key. Nothing is sent until you choose a provider and ask a question.
